__Impcat ionization__ is a process in a material where a single high-energy charge carrier loses energy to generate additional charge carriers. In semiconductors, it acts as the exact inverse mechanism of [[Auger recombination]].

## Key Concepts
- <b>Mechanism</b>: When a strong electric field accelerates a charge carrier (such as an electron or hole), it gains sufficient kinetic energy. Uppon colliding with a bound electron in the valance band, it knocks the electron into the conduction band, creating a new __electron-hole pair__.
- <b>Prerequisites</b>: Requires a sufficiently high electric field to accelerate the primary charge carriers to high kinetic energies, though it does not necessarily require a large current.

## Key Applications & Consequences
- <b>Avalanche Breakdown</b>: If impact ionization occurs continuously in a region with a high electric field, the rapid multiplication of free carriers leads to an avalanche breakdown.
- <b>Avalanche Diodes & Photodiodes (APDs)</b>: Used in optical receivers to amplify weak photocurrent signals before they encounter thermal circuit noise, significantly boosting sensitivity.
- <b>Cosmic Dust Detectors</b>: Employed in space instruments (such as the _Galileo, Cassini, and Stardust_ dust analyzers) to detect hypervelocity dust particle impacts and analyze their chemical composition.
