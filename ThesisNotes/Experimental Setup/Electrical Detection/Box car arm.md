In this note we discuss the analog arm of the box carr averager.
![[BoxCar.pdf]]
Above is the full schematic of a single one of the boxcar's arms. Here the signal passes through $3$ stages. The first stage is a trans-impedance amplifier bringing a micro-amp singal to about $10\,\text{mV}$$. The second stage is an RF-switch connected to a slow integrator, used to gate the and stretch the signal.

# Stage 1 (Trans Impedance Amplification)
The first stage is a pre-amplifier based on the Texas Instruments OPA855 (Bandwidth: $8\,\text{GHz}$). Powered on $\pm5\,\text{V}$, with decoupling capacitors $0.1\,\mu\text{F}$ decoupling power rail noise to ground. The feedback resistor $R_\text{f}$ is used to shunt the small current into a more manageable voltage:
$$
V_{\text{out}}=-I_{\text{in}}R_{\text{f}} = \left(1\,\mu\text{A}\right)\cdot\left(10\,\text{k}\Omega\right)=10\,\text{mV}
$$
The small feedback capacitor $C_{\text{f}}=0.1\,\text{pF}$ is used as a decoupling of the input node impedance to keep the amplifier stable, while maintaining bandwidth. The first stage is then AC coupled into the rest of the arm by use of a $100\,\text{nF}$ capacitor.
![[BoxCarStage1.png]]

# Stage 2 (Gating and Integration)
The second stage consists of an ADG901 GaAs RS-switch and an ADA4522 op-amp. The RF-switch is triggered using the [[Trigger generation]] circuit and is connected between $5\,\text{V}$ and ground, again with a $0.1\,\mu\text{F}$ decoupling capacitor between the power lines. By staying open for about $100\,\text{ns}$ every millisecond the switch rejects most of the DC and low frequency noise sources and drift. The op-amp is connected between $\pm5\,\text{V}$ with the same decoupling capacitors. The feedback components of the make it act like a pulse stretcher/ integrator with the formula,
$$
\Delta V_{\text{out}}=-\frac{1}{R_{\text{f}}C_{\text{f}}}\int_0^{2\tau}dt\,V_{\text{TIA}}(t)=-\frac{1}{\left(0.1\,\text{ms}\right)}\int_0^{\left(100\,\text{ns}\right)}dt\,V_{\text{TIA}}(t)
$$
	The output of the slow integrator is then passed through a passive lowpass filter with a cut-off frequency: $f_{\text{c}}=1/\left[2\pi\left(22\,\Omega\right)\cdot\left(0.1\,\mu\text{F}\right)\right]\approx72.3\,\text{kHz}$. This lowpass filter serves three purposes. It rejects high-frequency switching noise from the RF-switch, prevents aliasing by limiting the bandwidth to comply with the sample rate of the ADC and absorbs kick-back charge injection from the sampling.![[BoxCarStage2.png]]

# Stage 3 (Conversion to digital)
The third and final stage of the box car arm is a 16-bit precision SAR ADC. it converts the stable analog voltage generated by the integration stage onto a high-precision digital value that can be read out by a micro-controller. This ADC chip has bipolar input handling allowing for negative voltages, a high input impedance ($X_{\text{in}}\approx1\,\text{M}\Omega$) ensuring that change is not drained during sampling, 16-bit gives a resolution of $76,29\,\mu\text{V}$.
