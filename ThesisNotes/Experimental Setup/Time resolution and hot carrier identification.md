- Time is not measured by the electrical circuit but by the light pulses.
- The electrical circuit is used as an __Energy to Charge__ converter.

#### 1. The sample is hit by two light pulses separated by a time difference $\Delta t$.
#### 2. The nonlinear interaction "Hot carrier fingerprint"
   - __pulse 1__: excited hot carriers for $35-100\,\text{fs}$, these are the physical hot carriers with modified mobility $\mu_{\text{hot}}$, differing state-filling effects _Pauli blocking_, or some other change.
   - __Pulse 2__ arrives at delay time $\Delta t$:
	   - __if $\Delta t = 0$ (pulses overlap)__: Pulse 2 hits a system populated by hot carriers. State filling causes absorption saturation (or enhanced absorption dpending on the material), changing how many new carriers Pulse 2 generates or how they scatter.
	   - __if $\Delta t >> 100\,\text{fs}$ (long delay)__: The hot carriers generated by pulse 1 have already cooled down to the band edge (via optical phonon emission). Pulse 2 now interacts with a "cold" carrier distribution.
#### 3. Integrating the total charge
Because the response to Pulse 2 depends nonlinearly on the presence of hot carriers from Pulse 1, the __total collected photocurrent $I(\Delta t)$__ varres as a function of the optical delay $\Delta t$:
$$
\Delta I_{\text{PC}}(\Delta t)=I_{|text{PC}}(\Delta t) -\left[I_{\text{Pulse1}}+I_{\text{Pulse2}}\right]
$$
Even though the current flows through the wires over microseconds or milliseconds, the __total integrated charge collected per pulse pair__ changes depending on whether Pulse 2 saw hot carriers or cold carriers.

#### Possible mechanisms for the hot carrier signature
1. __State-filling / Pauli Blocking__: Hot carriers occupy high-energy states. Pulse 2 cannot excite electrons into these occupied states, reducing the total absorbed energy and creating a __dip in the photocurrent__ around $\Delta t = 0$.
2. __Carrier-Carrier Scattering & Impact Ionization__: High-energy carriers can collide with ground-state electrons to create additional electron-hole pairs before cooling. The second pulse modifies this multi-carrier interaction rate.
3. __Hot Carrier Transport & Extraction Barriers__: In devices with a contact/Schottky barrier, only "hot" carriers have enough energy to cross. Pulse 2 modifies the internal electric field or temperature gradient while carriers are still hot, directly altering extraction efficiency

# Photocurrent correlation math
Photocurrent correlation spectroscopy relies on converting a __time-resolved optical interaction__ into an __integrated DC electrical charge ($Q$).

The total collected photocurrent $I_{\text{DC}}(\tau)$ measured on a lock-in aplifier or picoammeter as a function of the inter-pulse delay time $\tau$ is given by,
$$
I_{\text{DC}}=f_{\text{rep}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\, J(t,\tau),
$$
where $f_{\text{rep}}$ is the laser repetition rate, and $J(t,\tau)$ is the instantaneous photogenerated current density. This current is produced by the combined electric field of the two pulses:
$$
E_{\text{total}}(t,\tau)=E_1(t)+E_2(t-\tau)
$$
#### 1. Perturbative Expansion of Photogenerated Charge
In any semiconductor device, the photogenerated current density $J(t,\tau)$ can be expressed as a function of the local carrier density $n(t,\tau)$ and their energy/mobility distribution. Expanding $J$ in powers of the optical intensity $I(t)\propto\left|E(t)\right|^2$:
$$
J(t,\tau)=J^{(1)}(t,\tau)+J^{(2)}(t,\tau)+\mathbf{O}(I^3)
$$
- __First-Order (Linear Response)__: $J^{(1)}(t,\tau)\propto I_1(t)+I_2(t-\tau)$.
- __Second-Order (Nonlinear Response)__: $J^{(2)}(t,\tau)$ accounts for carrier-carrier intercations, absorption saturation (bleaching), and hot-carrier mobility enhancement.

Integrating $J(t,\tau)$ over time gives the total charge $Q(\tau)$ collected per pulse pair,
$$
Q(\tau)=Q_1+Q_2+\Delta Q(\tau),
$$
where $Q_1=\int J^{(1)}(t)dt$ is the charge generates by pulse 1 in isolation, $Q_2=\int J^{(1)}(t-\tau)dt$ is the charge generated by pulse 2 in isolation and $\Delta Q(\tau)$ is the __nonlinear correlation charge__, which carries the hot-carrier dynamics.

#### 2. Derriving Hot-Carrier Dynamics ($\Delta Q(\tau)$)
Hot carriers relax via two main channels on ultrafast timescales:
1. __Carrier Energy Relaxation (Cooling)__: Energy distribution thermalizes to the lattice via optical phonon emission ($10\,\text{fs}-1\,\text{ps}$).
2. __Carrier Density Recombination / Trapping__: Loss of total charge carriers ($1\,\text{ps}-1\,\text{ns}$).

Let $n_{\text{h}}(t)$ be the density of hot carriers with energy $E > E_{\text{c}}+k_{\text{B}}T_0$ created by pulse 1. These hot carriers relax following a multi-rate kinetic equation,
$$
\frac{dn_{\text{h}}}{dt}=G_1(t)-\frac{n_{h}(t)}{\tau_{hot}},
$$
where $G_1(t)=\alpha_1I_1(t)\hbar\omega$ is the generation rate from pulse 1, and $\tau_{hot}$ is the hot-carrier cooling rate / lifetime  ($35\,\text{fs}-100\,\text{fs}$).

Solving for $n_{\text{h}}(t)$ by Green's function convolution gives,
$$
n_{\text{h}}=\int_{-\infty}^tdt'\,G_1(t')\exp\{-(t-t')/\tau_{\text{hot}}\}.
$$
When pulse 2 arrives at time $t=\tau$, it generates an additional current response proportional to the state of the material at that instant.

#### Mechanism A. Absorption saturation / Pauli Bleaching
Hot carriers generated by pulse 1 fill high-energy states, reducing the absorption coefficient $\alpha(t)$ seen by pulse 2,
$$
\alpha_2(t,\tau)=\alpha_0\left[1-\frac{n_{\text{h}}(t,\tau)}{N_{\text{sat}}}\right],
$$
where $N_{\text{sat}}$ is the saturation carrier density. The photogenerated charge from pulse 2 becomes,
$$
Q_2(\tau)\propto\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\,I_2(t-\tau)\left[1-\frac{n_{\text{h}}(t)}{N_{\text{sat}}}\right].
$$
Substituting the previous expression for $n_{\text{h}}(t)$ gives the nonlinear correlation term:
$$
\Delta Q(\tau)\propto-\frac{\alpha_0}{N_{\text{sat}}\hbar\omega}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\,I_2(t-\tau)\left[\int_{-\infty}^{t}dt'\,I_1(t')\exp\{-(t-t')/\tau_{\text{hot}}\}\right].
$$

#### Mechanism B. Hot-Carrier Mobility & Field Drift
If hot carriers have a transient mobility $\mu_{\text{hot}}=\mu_0+\Delta\mu_{\text{h}}\exp\{-t/\tau_{\text{hot}}\}$ higher than cold carriers,
$$
J(t)=qn(t)\mu(t)\mathcal{E}_{\text{internal}},
$$
the combined current trace generates an excess positive photocurrent spike $\Delta Q(\tau) >0$ centered around $\tau=0$.

#### 3. Evaluating the correlation integral
Assuming Gaussian laser pulses with temporal duration $\sigma_p$,
$$
I(t)=I_0\exp\{-t^2/2\sigma_p^2\},
$$
if $\sigma_p << \tau_{\text{hot}}$ (the laser pulse is much shorter than the hot-carrier lifetime), $I_1(t)$ acts like a delta functions $\delta(t)$. The double integral in the nonlinear charge term simplifies to,
$$
\Delta Q(\tau)\propto-\frac{\alpha_0I_0}{N_{\text{sat}}\hbar\omega}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\,I_2(t-\tau)\exp\{-t/\tau_{\text{hot}}\}=-\frac{\alpha_0I_0^2}{N_{\text{sat}}\hbar\omega}\exp\{-\tau/\tau_{\text{hot}}\},
$$
for $t > 0$. Due to indistinguishably of the two pulses $\Delta Q(\tau)$ must be symmetric constraining the function to be,
$$
\Delta Q(\tau)\propto A_0\exp\{-\tau/\tau_{\text{hot}}\}.
$$
For realistic pulse width where $\sigma_p\approx\tau_{\text{hot}}$, $\Delta Q(\tau)$ is given by the convoutoin of the optical intensity autocorrelation $A_I(\tau)$ with hot-carrier response function $R_{hc}(t)=C_0\exp\{-t/\tau_{\text{hot}}\}$,
$$
\Delta Q(\tau) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt'\,A_I(t'-t)R_{\text{hc}}(t').
$$
Solving the integral,
$$
\Delta Q(\tau)\propto-\frac{\alpha_0}{N_{\text{sat}}\hbar\omega}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\,I_2(t-\tau)\left[\int_{-\infty}^tdt'\,A_0\exp\{-t'^2/2\sigma_p^2\}\exp\{-(t-t')/\tau_{\text{hot}}\}\right],
$$
we first solve the inner integral from $-\infty$ to $t$.
$$
\int_{-\infty}^tdt'\,A_0\exp\{-t'^2/2\sigma_p^2\}\exp\{-(t-t')/\tau_{\text{hot}}\}=A_0\exp\{-t'/\tau_{\text{hot}}\}\int_{-\infty}^{t}dt'\,\exp\left\{-\frac{t'^2}{2\sigma_p^2}+\frac{t'}{\tau_{\text{hot}}}\right\}.
$$
$$
=A_0\exp\{-t'/\tau_{\text{hot}}\}\int_{-\infty}^tdt'\,\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2\sigma_p^2}\left(t'^2-\frac{2\sigma_p^2}{\tau_{\text{hot}}}t'\right)\right\},
$$
$$
=A_0\exp\left\{\frac{\sigma_p^2}{2\tau_{\text{hot}}^2}-\frac{t}{\tau_{\text{hot}}}\right\}\int_{-\infty}^tdt'\,\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2\sigma_p^2}\left(t'-\frac{\sigma_p^2}{\tau_{\text{hot}}}\right)^2\right\},
$$
now we switch to the variable $\eta=t'-\sigma_p^2/\tau_{\text{hot}}$, where $d\eta/dt'=1$, which gives,
$$
A_0\exp\left\{\frac{\sigma_p^2}{2\tau_{\text{hot}}^2}-\frac{t}{\tau_{\text{hot}}}\right\}\int_{-\infty}^{t+\sigma_p^2/\tau_{\text{hot}}}d\eta\,\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2\sigma_p^2}\eta^2\right\}.
$$
We now switch variables again to $\chi=\eta/\sqrt{2}\sigma_p$, where $d\eta=\sqrt{2}\sigma_pd\chi$.
$$
\sqrt{2}\sigma_pA_0\exp\left\{\frac{\sigma_p^2}{2\tau_{\text{hot}}^2}-\frac{t}{\tau_{\text{hot}}}\right\}\int_{-\infty}^{\chi_0}d\chi\exp\left\{-\chi^2\right\},
$$
$$
=\sqrt{2}\sigma_pA_0\exp\left\{\frac{\sigma_p^2}{2\tau_{\text{hot}}^2}-\frac{t}{\tau_{\text{hot}}}\right\}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\chi\exp\left\{-\chi^2\right\}-\int_{\chi_0}^{\infty}\exp\left\{-\chi^2\right\}\right],
$$
$$
=\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_pA_0\exp\left\{\frac{\sigma_p^2}{2\tau_{\text{hot}}^2}-\frac{t}{\tau_{\text{hot}}}\right\}\left[1-errf\left(t-\frac{\sigma_p^2}{\tau_{\text{hot}}}\right)\right]=f(t),
$$
with which the expression for the nonlinear charge becomes
$$
\Delta Q(\tau)\propto-\frac{\alpha_0}{N_{\text{sat}}\hbar\omega}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\,A_0\exp\left\{t^2/2\sigma_p^2\right\}f(t)
$$
