
Bakulin, A. A. _et al._ The Role of Driving Energy and Delocalized States for Charge Separation in Organic Semiconductors. _Science_ **335**, 1340–1344 (2012).

The objective of this work is to investigate the mechanism of long-range charge seperation in organic photovoltaic (OPV) hetrojunctions and determine wheter bound interfacial charge-transfer (CT) states dissociate via hot-state delocalization, through transient band states or through thermalized intermolecular hopping.

## Methodology
- <b>Approach</b>: Electro-optical pump-push photocurrent ($\delta PC/PC$) spectroscopy comined with three-pulse all-optical pump-push-probe transient absorption and anisotropy measurements.
- <b>Experimental setup</b>:
	- <b>Pump-push photocurrent</b>: A visible ($580\,\text{nm}$ or sub-gap $800\,\text{nm}$) pump pulse creates bound charge transfer (CT) states at low fluences $\approx10^{16}\,\text{cm}^{-3}$, followed bu a delayed mid-IR push pulse ($\approx0.5\,\text{eV}$) that selectively re-excites localized hole polarons.
	- <b>Pump-push-probe anisotropy</b>: A visible ($580\,\text{nm}$) pump, mid_IR push ($2\,\text{ps}$ delay), and mid-IR probe ($3\,\mu\text{m}$) monitor ultrafast isotropic population relaxation and polarization memory loss.
- <b>Materials tested</b>: A broad collection of polymer:fullerene and polymer:polymer blends ranging in external quantum efficiency ($\text{EQE}$) from $<6\%$ to $>80\%$ (e.g., PFB:F8BT, P3HT:F8TBT, MDMO-PPV:$\text{PC}_{70}\text{BM}$, P3HT:$\text{PC}_{60}\text{BM}$, PCPDTBT:$\text{PC}_{70}\text{BM}$, and PCDTBT:$\text{PC}_{70}\text{BM}$).
- <b>Theoretical modeling</b>: Miroelectrostatic atomistic modeling and quantum-chemical excited-state calculations of donor-acceptor interfaces evaluating hole wave function delocalization.

## Key findings
- <b>Delocalization gateway</b>: Absorption of a mid-IR push photon promotes localized, bound CT-state hole polarons into higher-lying delocalized valence band states ($CT_0\rightarrow CT_n$), increasing the average electron-hole spatial seperation on a sub-picosecond scale ($<100\,\text{fs}$).
- <b>Second-chance dissociation</b>: Mid-IR re-excitation probides bound CT-states a "second change" to dissociate with efficiencies ($\approx50\,\%$) comparable to initial exciton splitting.
- <b>Independence from exciton excess energy</b>: Sub-gap excitation (bypassing singlet exciton dissociation with $\sim 0.5\text{ eV}$ less energy) produced nearly identical pump-push photocurrent kinetics, proving that large exciton excess energy is not required for charge separation.- <b>EQE inverseness</b>: Low-efficiency systems (e.g., PFB:F8BT) exhibited massive photocurrent boosts ($10\text{--}50\%$) upon push excitation due to a large pool of trapped CT states, whereas high-efficiency systems (e.g., PCDTBT:$\text{PC}_{70}\text{BM}$) showed negligible or negative responses because charges dissociate naturally without trapping.

## Limitations
- <b>Transient lifetime bounds</b>: The optically induced delocalized states are extremely short-lived ($<1\,\text{ps}$) before decaying back into localized polarons or free separated charges.
- <b>Polaron trap bottleneck</b>: Once charges undergo structural relaxation to form localized polarons, their binding energy increases significantly, requiring additional energy inputs (like IR photons) to escape.
- <b>Material specificity</b>: The extent of hole wave function delocalization varies drastically between donor polymers and acceptor architectures, limiting generizability across unstudied blend combinations.
