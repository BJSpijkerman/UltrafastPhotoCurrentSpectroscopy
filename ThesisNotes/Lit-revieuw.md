## Introduction
 Ultrafast spectroscopy with photocurrent detection (USPD) is a well developed method for measuring the photo induced electronic currents. The advantage these techniques have over older transient absorption (TA) techniques is that due to the obervable of choise being a photocurrent, the technique is only sensitive to those processes that actually generate charge carriers. This combined with the ultrafast time resolution of femto-second laser pulses and the great sensitivity of electronic detection makes USPD ideal for investigating the charge transport processes in optoelectronic and photovoltaic devices.<br>
In this work we give an overvieuw of several of these techniques as applied to photovoltaic samples and compare the specific processes they probe. We then discuss the how these techniques have been used to bound carrier dissociation, electron-phonon coupling, ultrafast carrier transport, hot carrier physics, photothermal dynamics and phononic heat bottlenecks in various materials.<br>
We end this work by introducing two sets of photocurrent correlation experiments one performed on gold (AU) and one performed on <b>Some polymer</b> and introduce expected results.

## Ultrafast Spectroscopy with Photocurrent Detection
To elucidate carrier generation and transport in photovoltaic devices one first requires a device structure with an activate layer, cathode/ anode. In all these experimental procedures the active layer is illuminated with a series of ultrafast laser pulses, and the generated potential difference/ current is measured between the cathode and anode. The simplest version of such a setup is the so called push-push photocurrent, which consists of two interferometrically seperated pulses which illuminate the smaple and generate hot carriers. form the resulting photocurrent one can then obtain information about charge generation and transport phenomena.<br>
