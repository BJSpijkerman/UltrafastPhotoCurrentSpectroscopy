
Bakulin, A. A., Silva, C. & Vella, E. Ultrafast Spectroscopy with Photocurrent Detection: Watching ExcitonicOptoelectronic Systems at Work. _The Journal of Physical Chemistry Letters_ **7**, 250–258 (2016).

The aim of this work by Bakulin <i>et all</i> is to review the state of ultrafast spectroscopy methods using photocurrent detection, such as two-pulse photocurrent (2PPC) and multidimensional photocurrent (2DPC) and interferometric methods such as (PPPC). The paper establishes these types of methods as a superior alternative to more traditional transient absorption (TA) methods for the probing of carrier dynamics, charge-transfer pathways and vibronic coupling inside optoelectronic devices. This statement is based due to the direct measurement of the generated photocurrent, which is not sensitive to other optical processes that do not contribute to carrier generation to which the aforementioned (TA) methods are sensitive.

>These techniques have been used in the visible, mid-IR and THz range with electrical and tunneling current detection and are applicable to photovoltaic materials like pentacene single crystals and quantum dots.

## Key findings
- <b>Selective sensitivity</b>: unlike all-optical methods photocurrents are sensitive only to the subensemble of states that actually contribute to charge generation, where the all-optical method are also sensitive to non-conductive excitations and scattering.
- <b>Vibronic control</b>: Mid-IR push pulses revealed that resonant excitation of specific molecular vibrations ($1445\,\textit{cm}^{-1}$ along the long-axis in pentacene) selectively accelerates charge-hopping rates and increases photocurent output
- <b>Working-Condition Compatibility</b>: PC detection techniques operate under low to solar-equivalent photon fluxes this reduces high intensity artifacts, photodegradation and eliminate optical scattering and reflection issues
- <b>Device requirement</b>: These techniques require photovoltaic, electrically conductive devices or specialized nano-junctions equiped with contacts. This makes them insutable for isolated solution-phase or non-conductive bulk samples.
- <b>Interpretation complexity</b>: Since the photocurrent is sensitive to all charge producing processes one measures the collective contribution of each process rather than straightforward population densities. This requires careful theoretical modeling of thermalization and recombination pathways.
